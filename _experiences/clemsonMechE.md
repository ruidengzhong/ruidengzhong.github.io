As a Research Intern in Clemson University’s Department of Mechanical Engineering with Dr. Hai Xiao, I used atomistic molecular dynamics simulations to investigate the behavior of C₆₀ fullerene clusters under compression. I modeled face-centered cubic C₆₀ lattice structures and studied how temperature, interatomic potentials, and shear compression affected their stability, deformation, and energy response.

My work focused on exploring whether controlled compression could produce a carbon structure resembling long-range ordered porous carbon (LOPC). Although the simulations did not produce LOPC, they revealed a notable lattice-relaxation mechanism in which the upper portion of the FCC C₆₀ structure shifted into the spaces between lower-layer fullerenes, forming a stable deformed configuration.

The experience was completed at ASME's IMECE 2024 in Portland, Oregon, where I presented my findings during a poster session.

### Some of my work included:

- Creating and running molecular dynamics simulations of FCC C₆₀ lattice structures
- Investigating the effects of temperature, shear compression, and different interatomic potentials
- Optimizing C++ simulation programs to reduce runtime by 17%
- Running simulations on Clemson University’s Palmetto 2 high-performance computing cluster
- Using LAMMPS to configure and execute atomistic simulations
- Visualizing molecular structures and deformation using VMD
- Developing Python workflows to analyze simulation outputs and logs
- Evaluating temperature, volume, potential energy, shear deformation, and normal and shear stresses
- Identifying anomalous structural behavior and using simulation results to guide future studies
